# Editable summary templates

Daybook includes twelve original formats: meeting notes, 1:1, daily standup, therapy reflection, personal conversation, interview, lecture, planning, retrospective, customer discovery, design review, and decision memo. They specify what to retain and how to organize it. They do not add facts to the recording.

The template editor supports changing a built-in format, duplicating one, creating a custom format, removing a custom format, and resetting a built-in to its original instructions. Edits save when the user chooses Save, switches templates, or closes the editor. Each conversation can select a template ID; refreshing the summary applies that format while retaining the original notes, transcript runs, and summary history through the application's existing processing flow.

`SummaryTemplate` contains a stable string ID, name, base `MeetingKind`, instructions, and update date. `SummaryTemplate.catalog(templates:)` merges saved templates with defaults and keeps the newest version of duplicate IDs. `resolved(id:kind:templates:)` uses an explicit selection first, then the default for the detected conversation type. A removed or unavailable custom ID falls back to that conversation type. Removing a built-in override restores the bundled original. `duplicated()` assigns a new custom identity.

The app stores edited templates in its library settings. This module makes no network requests. Root application settings/synchronization own persistence; the editor uses `AppState.templates`, `saveTemplate(_:)`, and `removeTemplate(_:)`.

Useful templates describe the purpose and context, requested detail and tone, and the sections to produce. This structure follows the public guidance in [Granola's template documentation](https://docs.granola.ai/help-center/taking-notes/customise-notes-with-templates); Daybook's included prompt wording is original. Test a format on an existing conversation and refine its instructions based on the resulting notes.

Therapy reflection is a record of the conversation, using the participant's own account and explicitly discussed practices. Its prompt avoids inventing diagnoses, treatment plans, or explanations of motives. Every format asks the model to preserve uncertainty, supported attribution, and concrete commitments. User notes and marked moments should receive additional relevant context. Model output still requires review, and a template cannot reconstruct missing audio or resolve unknown speakers.

## Notes and bookmarks

A summary is written from the transcript with the person's notes and bookmarks in it, not beside it (`SummarySource` in DaybookKit, `MeetingSynthesis` in DaybookIntelligence):

- **In place.** Each note paragraph with a time and each bookmark on the timeline becomes a line of its own at its time: `[01:18] NOTE N2, written by the user (not spoken): Budget cap is firm at 40k`, `[02:11] BOOKMARK B2, marked by the user: Pricing decision`. Notes written before or after the recording, and bookmarks on a recording that isn't lined up (with that recording's own passages), are listed with every note as `user_notes_and_bookmarks`, each with its label, kind, time or null, and text.
- **More weight around them.** Passages from a minute before to 15 seconds after a note (a note is usually written just after what it's about), and from 30 seconds before to a minute after a bookmark, are marked `(near N2)`. Every prompt asks for those passages in more detail.
- **Kept when trimmed.** In a conversation too long for one pass, each part's extraction sees the notes in its part, and every note when they fit in 1,500 tokens, and records what was said around each one under its label, then only what a note adds that no speaker said ("N1 adds: …") or a conflict ("N1 conflicts: …"); a bookmark's words only name the moment and are never recorded as a fact. Compression keeps facts tied to their labels, and the final pass gets the notes plus up to 1,400 tokens of the marked passages word for word. Those are chosen round by round: every note and bookmark with words gets its nearest passage (for a note, the one before it) before any gets a second, then bare bookmarks.
- **Checked first** (Gemma 4 26B A4B and 31B, Qwen 3.6 35B A3B: the catalog's `noteCheck: "on"`). Before anything is written, one pass compares each note on the timeline with what was said from two minutes before it to 30 seconds after (`MeetingSynthesis.notesRepeatingWhatWasSaid`, `SummarySource.passages(around:)`): what the passages say about it, what the note adds that no passage says, and where it conflicts. A note found to add nothing and conflict with nothing goes on to every later pass without its words, as a moment the person marked (`[02:10] NOTE N1, written by the user, repeats what was said around it`), so no pass can give it "Your notes add"; its passages still carry more weight. The rest are compared by the summary as below: given the check's findings too, Gemma 4 26B wrote more notes as the notes' and one as a fact. Asked only to compare while writing the summary, the 26B and 12B gave notes that repeat what was said "Your notes add". On the other models the check called notes that add something repeats too (Gemma 4 12B 2 of 11, E4B 4, E2B 8, Qwen 3.5 9B 2, 4B 6, Qwen 2.5 7B all 11), and those notes would lose their words, so they compare every note as below. A note written outside the recording, or a check that can't be read, is compared as before.
- **Folded in.** The final pass is told to fold each note into the section it belongs to, read with the conversation around it, never to list notes on their own, under their own heading, or at the end, and never to copy them word for word. It compares each note with what was said around it: what the conversation also says, in any words, is written from the conversation, without mentioning the note (most notes repeat what was said); what only the note says, which no speaker said (a fact, a date, a name, a source such as an email, an impression), goes in its section as the person's ("Your notes add that the contract was signed Monday, per legal"), never as something said, decided, or confirmed, and "Your notes add" is only for this; a conflict with what was said is mentioned. A bookmark's words only name the moment it marks: what was said there is never written as the person's notes. In a long conversation, each part's extraction and each compression keep what a note adds marked as the note's. Notes are never a speaker's words, and commitments are checked only against the recorded transcript.

A template's instructions still choose the sections; the notes go into those sections. Inference that only takes strings (`MeetingInference.summarize(transcript:notes:…)`) still gets the transcript, notes, and bookmark evidence it always did.

Tests cover stable defaults, unique IDs, editing and duplicate resolution, explicit selection, missing-ID fallback, independent copies, and Unicode/multiline persistence. `SummaryNotesTests` cover the notes' windows and placement, trimming, the note check (a note that adds nothing passed on without its words), the prompts in short and long conversations (each note compared with what was said, what only a note says kept the note's through extraction and compression), templates with notes, and that a note can't stand as a commitment's quote. `SummaryCompletenessTests` cover a long conversation's parts read whole (a reply cut off at its limit is read again in halves), commitments checked in parts against the words said, owners from the quote and its speaker, and sentences a quotation mark can't cut short. SummaryRepairTests cover what a smaller model's reply keeps when it loops or breaks off, and that notes copied with their markers are left out. How a real model follows them is in [validation](validation.md) (2026-10-30).
